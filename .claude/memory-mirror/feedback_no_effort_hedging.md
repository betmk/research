Don't ask "want me to do X? it's a Y-minute edit" or otherwise weigh effort/time before acting. The user explicitly said: *"I don't worry. I'm not concerned about how long it takes or how much effort you think it'd require for me or you. We just want to do this the best way possible."*

**How to apply:**
- When I find issues that violate the user's stated criteria, fix them — don't surface as a question gated by "would you like me to."
- Don't size proposals by effort ("2-minute edit" / "10-minute write"). Just do the work.
- When the right answer is the more thorough one, take that path.
- This applies across the research project: refreshes, fixes, methodology updates, formatting, etc.

**Why:** User's bandwidth isn't the bottleneck — quality is. They'd rather I do the right thing than ask permission for incremental work that's clearly aligned with what they asked for. Asking about effort routes around the actual decision and creates churn.
