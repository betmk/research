When attributing quotes to Sparta sources in the Hormuz report, distinguish three tiers of evidence:

1. **Verbatim audio podcast (Trade with Conviction):** NOT accessible via WebFetch. Podbean/Apple/Spotify pages give episode metadata + chapter titles + show descriptions, but no transcript. Don't write "Crosby said X on Ep Y" unless the user has provided the verbatim quote.

2. **Verbatim chapter titles from podcast platforms:** Accessible. These ARE Sparta's own framing. Examples from Ep 92 (May 7): "US diesel stocks: weeks of runway left" (chapter 2), "Middle distillates: East-West looks too cheap" (chapter 5), "a reckoning may be only weeks away" (episode description). Citable as "Sparta Ep X chapter title: '…'".

3. **Sparta written Deep Dives (Market Outlook):** Fully accessible via WebFetch. Always check `spartacommodities.com/market-outlook/` for same-week written companions to podcast episodes. Examples: May 7 Noel-Beswick "Global jet and diesel pricing should continue to be bearish…", May 11 Crosby "Time for physical to shine". These have full verbatim text and are citable directly.

**Why:** User on 2026-05-11 caught me conflating "Ep 92 reinforces" with "Ep 92 directly says" — most quotes I attributed to Ep 92 actually came from Crosby's Apr 29 written piece "Surely we need to be long, but when?" The user pushed: "It's ridiculous you haven't analyzed the content of the podcast and are claiming to make suggestions based on it." Lesson sharpened from the recovery.

**How to apply:**
- On every Hormuz refresh, fetch `spartacommodities.com/market-outlook/` and `spartacommodities.com/insights/markets/distillate/` for new written content alongside the Podbean episode metadata.
- When citing in the report, label sources precisely: "Crosby, Sparta Deep Dive May 11" vs "Sparta Ep 92 chapter title (May 7)" vs "Crosby, Sparta Distillate written piece April 29". Never write "Sparta said" as a generic.
- If user asks for verbatim podcast audio, be upfront that WebFetch can't reach transcripts; offer to search wire pickups (Reuters/Al Jazeera/Bloomberg) which often quote analysts after episodes air.
